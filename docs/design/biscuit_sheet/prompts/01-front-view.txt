=== THE ATTACHED IMAGES ===

IMAGE 1 IS A CONSTRUCTION DIAGRAM, not a photograph and not a finished drawing. It is a
crude schematic of the exact drawing you must produce. It is authoritative for:
  - the PROPORTIONS: how big the head is against the body, how long the legs are, how
    wide the chest is, where the floor is;
  - the LAYOUT: where the eyes, the brow pips, the nose, the muzzle, the ears, the bib,
    the stockings, the feet and the tail sit;
  - the COLOURS: every fill in it is the exact colour that part of her must be;
  - the REGISTER: white keyline, flat fills, near-black ground, smooth outline.

Your job is to REDRAW IMAGE 1 as a skilled illustrator would: same animal, same size,
same position on the canvas, same proportions, same colours, same marking placement —
but properly drawn, with a confident continuous outline, believable dog anatomy at the
shoulder, elbow and hock, the legs reading as legs rather than as tubes, the coat
suggested at the edges, and the head and body flowing into one another instead of being
stacked shapes.

IMPORTANT: Image 1's outline is SMOOTH. Keep it smooth. Do NOT scallop it, do not turn
it into a chain of bumps, do not draw it as a row of curls, and do not add a white halo,
glow or offset sticker rim anywhere. A slight lazy waver in the line is all the coat gets.

THE REMAINING IMAGES ARE PHOTOGRAPHS OF THE REAL DOG. Take from them only the fine
character that a schematic cannot carry: the exact shape of her head and stop, the way
her ear hair falls, the shape of her nose, the irregular length of her coat. Take NOTHING
else from them — not the colour, not the light, not the background, not the collar or
harness or tag they show, and not the photographic style. Where a photograph and Image 1
disagree about proportion or about where a marking sits, IMAGE 1 WINS.

=== WHAT TO DRAW: THE FRONT VIEW OF A TURNAROUND ===

One dog, alone, standing square and facing the viewer head on, seen from her own eye
level so that the camera is level with her head and NOT looking down at her. Her whole
body is in frame from the top of the topknot to the bottom of all four feet, centred,
with a clear even margin on all four sides, exactly as Image 1 places her.

She is drawn straight on, upright and symmetrical: a technical model-sheet view, with no
perspective trick, no dynamic angle and no foreshortening.

Her mouth is closed — one short flat white stroke, no curve up and no curve down. Her
brow pips are two small level cream OVALS, not slanting eyebrows: she is deadpan, not
cross and not worried. Her eyes are small, round and level, looking straight at the
viewer.
