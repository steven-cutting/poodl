Front, three-quarter, profile, rear, at one scale. A square compact dog, head carried close, ears to the jaw. Nothing in the corpus measures her, so the ratios here are the bible's bounds, not measurements; the rear view's rump and back are body colour because the hindquarter markings are unverified; the tail is carried up and loosely curved by provision.

2 · Head studies provisional — head profile

A circle with a stub: domed skull, short blunt muzzle, soft stop. Ears set low at eye level, two lobes to the jawline, the darkest value on her. Topknot held clear of the eyes. The profile rests on a single filtered frame in the corpus and is flagged until the shoot confirms it.

3 · The fixed face fixed

The eyes are the face. Round, wide-set, open, level, on the viewer; amber iris, separate dark pupil, liver rim; the two brow pips level above them. Everything else is posture. Shown at hero size and at the minimum the bible permits, 180 × 135 — below that, only the mark appears.

4 · Marking map provisional — hindquartersprovisional — adolescent foot

Cream sits in five places and nowhere else

  1. Muzzle band — wrapping the muzzle from behind the nose back under the eyes.
  2. Brow pips — two, directly above the eyes; always drawn.
  3. Bib — chest and throat.
  4. Stockings — from about the elbow and the hock down.
  5. Feet — cream mops; pads dark, shown only when the sole turns.

Skull, ear leathers, back, flanks and rump are body colour. Until the shoot, nothing above the hock is cream. The adolescent foot is drawn from puppy frames and flagged.

6 · Line weight at three sizes

One weight per asset and the same weight on every asset: 2 user units on the 240 × 180 stage, which is 1.5 px — the interface's --rule-w-strong, drawn above each specimen — at the minimum size and scales with the drawing. She is a print, not a UI rule: at hero size the line is proportionally the same, not thinner.

7 · Posture vocabulary

The seven postures the backlog's first pose set draws from, each on the shared 4:3 stage. The face is the same in every one that shows it; only posture changes. The mouth opens at a run and a roll because a running or rolling dog's does, and that is posture, not expression. None of the seven exists yet: the image model's quota ran out after the panels above were drawn, so these slots are briefs, not drawings, until it resets.

8 · What is provisional on this sheet

  • Proportions — the bible's bounds, not measurements; the shoot's level full-body frames close them.
  • Tail length and carriage at rest — drawn up and loosely curved by provision.
  • Hindquarter and back markings — nothing above the hock is cream until a rear frame says otherwise.
  • The adolescent foot — every readable paw in the corpus is a puppy's.
  • The head profile — rests on a single red-filtered frame.
  • The iris value — --biscuit-5 is the ramp's nearest step to amber; if it reads orange here, one amber token is proposed.
  • Rendering — these panels are generated rasters. The fills approximate the tokens and the line approximates one weight; the shipped assets are vector, currentColor and token fills, and this sheet fixes what they draw, not how they are built.
