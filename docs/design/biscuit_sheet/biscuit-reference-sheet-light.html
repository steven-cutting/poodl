Head studies

The fixed face — the eyes are the face

When she faces the viewer both eyes are visible, round, open, level — neither narrowed nor widened — and looking at the viewer. The brow pips sit level above them. This does not change from one image to the next. Everything else is posture: the mouth may open at a run or a roll, the ears may lift or flatten, the head may tilt, and the body may do anything the moment calls for. Deadpan is the resting state. No image on this sheet breaks the face.

The drawings above were generated and cannot be authored to a viewBox, so the posture thumbnails are fitted into 4:3 frames with the floor line drawn — the stage as a reading aid, not as geometry.

Posture vocabulary

What is provisional on this sheet

  • Proportions. No frame in the corpus can measure leg length or body depth, and the age the sheet is anchored to has no whole-body frame at all. Every ratio here is inference from the breed's structure corrected by her frames.
  • The adolescent foot's shape. Documented as a puppy and as a grown dog, which agree about pad colour and feathering. The adolescent middle is not photographed.
  • The head profile. Rests on two frames that agree and neither of which is what the sheet wants: one square on with its colour destroyed, one in colour with the head turned.
  • The iris value. --biscuit-5 is the ramp's nearest step to amber. No full frame corroborates her iris; if this reads orange, one amber token is proposed.
  • The anchor age itself. Every whole animal in the folder is a grown dog whose build is heavier and shorter-legged than the adolescent this sheet is drawn to.

What the twenty full frames closed, and is no longer provisional

  • Tail carriage. Up and curved over the back standing; slack and laid out behind her settled. Closed by the twenty full frames.
  • The hindquarter markings. Back, flanks and rump carry no cream at all; the stockings begin at about the elbow and the hock. Closed by the two grown lying frames.
